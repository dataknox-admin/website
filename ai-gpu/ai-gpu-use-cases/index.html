Why GPU Architecture Matters

Match Your Workload to the Right GPU Infrastructure

Not all AI workloads are alike. Computer vision needs high-throughput Tensor Cores to process image and video data at scale. LLM training and fine-tuning demands maximum memory bandwidth and NVLink interconnects to move massive parameter counts between GPUs without bottlenecks. Autonomous driving simulation requires real-time ray tracing and inference at scale across sensor-fusion pipelines. Life sciences workloads — protein folding, genomics, radiology AI — need double-precision floating point and very large GPU memory footprints.

Dataknox helps engineering and ML teams select, deploy, and support the right GPU architecture for each use case. Whether you need NVIDIA GB200, H200, or H100; Intel Gaudi 2 or Gaudi 3; or AMD MI300X — Dataknox manages global logistics, on-site installation, and ongoing hardware support through 10,527 certified field engineers worldwide.

Frequently Asked Questions on AI GPU Use Cases

For computer vision, NVIDIA H100 and H200 GPUs deliver the highest throughput for real-time inference and training. Their Tensor Cores are optimized for the matrix operations central to convolutional neural networks and vision transformers. For real-time edge inference at scale, NVIDIA A10 and L40S are strong options. For very high-volume batch inference pipelines, AMD MI300X also performs well given its large HBM3 memory capacity.
Training requires maximum memory bandwidth, large GPU memory (80–192 GB per card), and high-speed interconnects like NVLink for multi-GPU jobs. NVIDIA H100, H200, and GB200 are purpose-built for this. Inference requires high throughput at lower latency and can often be served more economically on smaller GPUs like NVIDIA L40S or A10. If your team is doing both, Dataknox can structure a mixed-GPU deployment — training cluster plus inference nodes — sized to your workload and budget.
