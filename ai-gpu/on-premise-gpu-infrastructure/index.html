What It Means

What Is On-Premise
GPU Infrastructure?

On-premise GPU infrastructure means GPU compute hardware physically located in a facility you control — your own data center or a colocation space you lease. The hardware is dedicated to your workloads, not shared with other tenants, and the data never leaves your network perimeter.

What You Control

With on-premise GPU infrastructure, your team controls the hardware configuration, the network topology, the security policies, and the physical access. There is no cloud provider making decisions about your environment.

Data sovereignty is absolute: training datasets, model checkpoints, and inference traffic stay within your facility. This matters for regulated industries and any organization handling sensitive data.

Performance is consistent. You are not competing with other tenants for compute capacity, and you do not face the variable latency introduced by hyperscaler networking. Your GPU cluster delivers the same throughput every time you run a job.

The Economics

Cloud GPU is the right choice for bursty, unpredictable workloads. For sustained, high-utilization AI and ML workloads — model training runs that last weeks, inference endpoints that run around the clock — on-premise hardware typically reaches break-even with cloud GPU within 12 to 24 months.

Dataknox offers zero CapEx structures that let you deploy dedicated GPU hardware without a large upfront purchase. You get the performance and control of owned infrastructure with a predictable monthly cost.

Eliminating cloud egress fees is another factor. Moving large training datasets into a cloud GPU environment and pulling results back out generates egress costs that compound over time. On-premise removes that entirely.

Decision Framework

When On-Premise Beats
Cloud GPU

On-premise GPU is not the right answer for every situation. These are the conditions where it consistently outperforms cloud GPU.

Data Sovereignty

Regulations or internal policy require that training data, model weights, and inference traffic never leave a specific physical location or jurisdiction.

Regulated Industries

Financial services, healthcare, and government workloads often require on-premise GPU for compliance with HIPAA, FedRAMP, PCI-DSS, and similar frameworks.

Sustained High Utilization

Workloads that run GPU hardware at 60% or higher utilization continuously typically reach cloud GPU cost parity within 12 to 24 months on owned hardware.

Latency-Sensitive Inference

Real-time inference applications where round-trip latency to a cloud region introduces unacceptable delay benefit from GPU hardware in your own facility.

Large Data Training

Training runs that ingest or produce terabytes of data face significant cloud egress costs when data must move between your facility and the cloud GPU environment.

Security-First Architecture

Air-gapped environments, zero-trust network models, and workloads where hardware-level isolation is a requirement are best served by on-premise GPU.
