Service Coverage

Workstation Maintenance Services

Component coverage explains which parts Dataknox can replace, while service coverage explains the support you receive. This includes remote troubleshooting, on-site repair, replacement parts, depot repair, and hardware replacement.

Remote Hardware Troubleshooting

Diagnosis begins remotely. Engineers review error codes, diagnostic output, event logs, and failure symptoms to isolate the failed component before any part ships or any engineer travels. Remote diagnosis prevents incorrect parts dispatch and shortens total repair time.

On-Site Workstation Repair

Field engineers attend the site and complete the repair at the workstation’s physical location. Response timing follows the SLA attached to that specific asset, not a single blanket contract term.

Replacement Parts

Replacement hardware is sourced through the Dataknox parts network, which holds tested components for current and legacy workstation platforms. Independent sourcing keeps parts available for systems the manufacturer no longer stocks.

Depot Repair

The workstation ships to a Dataknox repair facility for diagnosis and component-level repair, then returns to site. Depot service suits secondary systems, spare units, and workstations where a same-day engineer visit is unnecessary.

Hardware Replacement

Failed components are replaced under the terms of the coverage agreement. Replacement scope, part class, and turnaround are defined in the contract at the point of signing.

Service Levels

Workstation Maintenance SLA Options

Dataknox offers 4-hour response, next-business-day support, depot service, and custom fleet coverage. You can assign different SLA levels to different workstations under one agreement.

You can mix SLA levels across the same workstation fleet. Critical systems can receive 4-hour support, while less important devices use next-business-day or depot service.

Hardware Lifecycle

Extend Workstation Life Beyond OEM Warranty and EoL

Third-party maintenance separates the hardware replacement date from the OEM support end date. A workstation that still meets its performance requirement stays in production under a TPM contract, regardless of where it sits in the manufacturer’s lifecycle schedule.

Six situations make extended coverage the correct decision:

  • Expired warranty: The standard 3-year term has ended and the system remains in daily use
  • Expensive renewal: The OEM extension quote exceeds the value of the remaining service life
  • Adequate performance: The workstation still runs its certified applications at acceptable speed
  • Unnecessary replacement: No workload change justifies new hardware
  • Delayed refresh: Budget cycles, supply lead times, or project schedules have pushed the refresh date
  • Legacy dependency: The system runs validated software, a licensed toolchain, or an instrument driver tied to that specific hardware
