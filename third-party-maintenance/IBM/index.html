OEM vs TPM

Which IBM Systems Should Stay With OEM Support?

Newer IBM hardware like Power11, Power10, and recent FlashSystem products need direct IBM access for software rights and vendor updates during the earlier years of a system’s service life.

IBM Power11 Systems

IBM Power11 includes the E1180 and E1150 enterprise systems. The family also includes S1124, S1122, and S1112 servers. IBM was still publishing Power11 firmware updates in July 2026.

IBM Power10 Systems

Power10 includes the E1080 and E1050 systems. Other models include S1024, S1022, S1014, and S1012. IBM publishes current IBM i compatibility information for Power10 hardware.

Recent IBM FlashSystem Models

Recent IBM FlashSystem models like C200, 5300, 7300, and 9500 are in IBM’s supported storage documentation. As these systems use IBM Storage Virtualize software, they may need IBM fixes.

TPM Candidates

Which IBM Systems Are Better Candidates for TPM?

POWER7 systems, POWER8 S824 servers, POWER9 S922 servers, System x3650 hardware, and FlashSystem 9200 arrays can work well with TPM. After years, these systems reach a point where hardware service matters more than the latest software features.

  • Older stable production systems: Older stable systems like IBM Power S824 can remain in service as long as their IBM i workload still runs well.
  • Systems past warranty: A POWER9 S922 can move to TPM after its original warranty or IBM service term ends.
  • EOS hardware: A POWER7 Power 770 can use TPM when standard OEM hardware service is no longer available.
  • Backup or secondary systems: A System x3650 can support secondary workloads without a higher-cost IBM hardware contract.
  • Hardware waiting for a planned refresh: A POWER9 E980 can stay covered while the company prepares a later move to Power11.
