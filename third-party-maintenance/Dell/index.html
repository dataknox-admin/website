OEM vs TPM

Which Dell Systems Should Stay With ProSupport?

Newer Dell systems need direct Dell access for firmware, platform updates, and active product support, and they should stay with ProSupport.

17th Generation PowerEdge Servers

Dell publishes BIOS and iDRAC10 updates for 17th generation PowerEdge servers. Current models include R470, R570, R670, R770, R7715, and R7725. These systems remain early in their hardware life and often need direct Dell update access.

Dell PowerEdge XE Systems

Recent PowerEdge XE systems target GPU and high-compute workloads that depend on vendor updates. Dell lists XE7740, XE7745, XE9780, and XE9785 among its 17th generation systems. New hardware has an active warranty value that TPM cannot replace. Direct Dell service makes more sense during this stage.

Current Dell PowerStore Systems

Dell currently sells PowerStore 1200T, 3200T, 5200T, and 9200T systems. The family also includes models such as 1500, 5500, and 9500. Dell provides active platform software and hardware support for these arrays.

Current Dell PowerScale Systems

Current PowerScale models include F910, F710, F210, H710, H7100, and A3100. These systems serve active file, AI, archive, and enterprise storage workloads. Software updates and platform support can still carry real value during their current product life.

PowerEdge R730, R740, R640, older PowerVault systems, and mature Dell EMC storage are strong TPM candidates. These products often reach a point where hardware coverage matters more than new vendor features.

Cost Analysis

When Does Dell ProSupport Stop Making Financial Sense?

Dell ProSupport isn’t helpful when an older system needs parts and hardware repair. A stable PowerEdge server may no longer need the same vendor coverage it needed when new. At that point, support costs should match the hardware’s remaining role.

TPM becomes more attractive after warranty expiry or when renewal pricing rises against the asset’s business value. It can also fit when software updates no longer matter to the workload. A planned refresh may be another reason to avoid a long OEM renewal.

Dataknox positions TPM as an alternative when Dell ProSupport expires or becomes too expensive. Its published TPM pricing is 30% to 85% below OEM renewal costs.

  • The hardware is past warranty or approaching EOL
