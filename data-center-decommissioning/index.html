How It Works

The Dataknox Decommission Process

Not the brochure version — here's what actually happens, step by step.

01

Discovery & Inventory

Full asset discovery using automated audit tooling — 7× faster than manual. Every asset tagged, catalogued, and entered into a project CMDB before physical work begins.

02

Project Planning

Scope definition, timeline, and vendor coordination. Colocation operators, equipment lessors, and facilities teams aligned. Chain-of-custody protocol locked before day one on-site.

03

Data Destruction

NAID AAA-certified destruction onsite or at our secure facilities. Every drive processed and video-recorded. Certificates of Destruction generated in real time — not retroactively.

04

Deinstall & Pack

Rack teardown, cable management, and packing by certified field engineers. Every asset logged against the project inventory at each handoff point.

05

Asset Disposition

Recoverable hardware assessed at fair market value within 48 hours. Buyback proceeds returned to client. End-of-life equipment recycled under R2v3 — zero landfill.

06

Compliance Closeout

Final project report: all CODs, chain-of-custody documentation, asset manifest, and recycling certificates. Delivered digitally through the client portal. Audit-ready on delivery.

Data Center Decommissioning FAQs

Data center decommissioning is the process of safely shutting down and clearing out a data center — deinstalling servers, storage, and networking equipment, destroying data on storage media to certified standards, recovering value from assets, and producing compliance documentation throughout. In practice it ranges from a single-floor shutdown to a multi-country exit coordinated across dozens of colocation facilities. The compliance documentation piece is often the part teams underestimate until they're sitting in front of an auditor.
A single-rack or single-floor project typically takes 2–4 weeks including planning and compliance closeout. A multi-site project across 10+ locations can run 8–16 weeks. That said, our fastest was the Instart Logic engagement — 22 sites across 9 countries, completed in 60 days during COVID-19 access restrictions. Timeline is driven by asset count, site access logistics, and colocation operator coordination, not arbitrary scheduling.
NAID AAA — the highest industry standard for data destruction, independently audited. R2v3 for responsible recycling. ISO 9001, ISO 14001, and ISO 45001. NIST 800-88 and DoD 5220.22-M compliance for media sanitization. All destruction is video-recorded. Certificates of Destruction are generated in real time during the destruction event — not produced retroactively. Those CODs are the compliance artifact that closes GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS, and SOX audit findings on data disposal.

Assets get triaged into three paths: destroyed (all storage media containing sensitive data, per NAID AAA standards), recovered (resaleable hardware assessed at fair market value within 48 hours — buyback proceeds returned to you), and recycled (end-of-life equipment under R2v3 zero-landfill certification). The buyback component is worth taking seriously. On a large decommission, recovered hardware value can offset a meaningful portion of the project cost.
